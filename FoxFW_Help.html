Chapter 1

About FoxFW

FoxFW is a fully custom Flipper Zero firmware that extends the official firmware with a large set of exclusive features. It is designed around three goals: security, usability, and deeper RF tooling.

📸 See it in action: Check out our collection of screenshots to get a feel for FoxFW before you dive in. Got some great shots of your own? FoxFW has a built-in Screenshot Tool (hold Back+Up on any screen) — send us what you capture and we may feature it here with a link to your own GitHub page!

Key things that make FoxFW different from stock firmware:

  • A multi-screen first-boot setup wizard that guides you through naming and securing your device.
  • A full PIN lock system with configurable attempt limits, automatic lock timers, and an On Exceed security action.
  • Granular Disconnect on Lock — choose whether BLE, GPIO, and USB are disabled when the screen locks.
  • A custom Fox File Browser (FFB) that replaces the stock Archive, with a Favorites system and firmware-install support.
  • System-wide bounce-scroll text — any label too long to fit bounces left and right automatically rather than wrapping or truncating.
  • A Modulation Analyzer exclusive to FoxFW — find what modulation an unknown device uses, then open the Receiver pre-configured for it.
  • An in-app RAW Waveform Editor for trimming Sub-GHz captures without leaving the device.
  • A live Waterfall Signal Display for spotting intermittent transmissions.
  • Per-protocol enable / disable toggles in the Protocol List.
  • An extended Custom Wallpaper system with a factory-seeded template that generates automatically if missing.
  • A Sub-Ghz Mode picker on launch — choose Automotive or Garage/Gate/Other, each opening the same familiar Sub-GHz screen scoped to that protocol set, plus dedicated rows for RF Jammer, TPMS Read/Edit, and Radio Settings.
  • A Sub-GHz Bruteforcer app in the App Menu for brute-forcing fixed-code remotes across common protocols.
  • A built-in Screenshot Tool — hold Back+Up on any screen to save what's currently displayed as a standard .xbm file, no computer or qFlipper connection required.
  • A full Fox ESP32 companion suite — FoxHub, Chat, Portal, Detector, Flasher, and Chameleon — turning a wired ESP32 into a WiFi/BLE/HTTP extension of your Flipper. Powered by the separate Fox ESP32 Firmware.

Chapter 2

Web Installer New

FoxFW can be installed directly from your browser — no software required. Connect your Flipper via USB and the web installer handles everything, including downloading the firmware and triggering the update.

Recommended for most users. Works in Chrome and Edge on desktop. Your Flipper must be connected via USB before you begin.
⚡ Open Web Installer →

Opens at foxfw.github.io/fox-web/flasher.html

How it works

  1. Open the link above in Chrome or Edge on desktop.
  2. Choose Standard Update (Flipper on) or DFU Recovery (Flipper unresponsive).
  3. Click Connect Flipper and select your device from the browser dialog.
  4. Click Install Now — the installer downloads the firmware and transfers it automatically.
  5. Your Flipper reboots and applies the update. The Fox Setup Wizard runs on first boot.
Standard Update uploads the firmware as a .tgz package to your SD card via the Flipper's USB connection, then triggers the install on reboot. Your files and settings are preserved.

DFU Recovery writes the firmware only directly to the Flipper's internal flash via WebUSB — use this if your Flipper will not boot normally. SD card contents are not affected. After a DFU Recovery, it is HIGHLY RECOMMENDED to complete a Standard Install - this will ensure that the core FoxFW apps are all returned to the device.

FAP Compiler New

Want to run a third-party app that isn't bundled with FoxFW? Paste its GitHub URL into the FAP Compiler and it builds a .fap against FoxFW v2.0 for you — no local toolchain install required.

No GitHub account or login needed — just paste a repo URL and compile.
⚙ Open FAP Compiler →

Opens at foxfw.github.io/fox-web/fap-compiler.html

How it works

  1. Paste the GitHub URL of the app you want, then click Compile .FAP.
  2. Watch the live console as GitHub Actions checks out FoxFW v2.0 and builds the app.
  3. Download the finished .fap and copy it to apps/ on your SD card.
The target repo just needs an application.fam file — the same format used by every Flipper Zero app. Multi-app repos are supported via an advanced "subfolder" option.

Chapter 3

First Boot — Fox Setup Wizard

After installing FoxFW (or after a firmware update that includes a wizard reset), the device automatically launches the Fox Setup Wizard on the first boot. You cannot skip the wizard entirely — you must at least reach the final page and press Finish.

The wizard walks through four pages:

  1. Welcome — Introduces FoxFW. Press Right or OK to continue.
  2. Rename your Flipper — Optionally set a custom device name.
  3. Set a PIN — Optionally secure the device with a lock-screen PIN.
  4. Done — Summary. If you made changes the device restarts automatically to apply them.
You can skip both name and PIN by pressing Left (Skip) on those pages. The wizard will still complete and the device will boot normally. You can configure both later in Fox Settings.

Renaming your Flipper

Enter a name using the on-screen keyboard. Allowed characters are letters (A–Z, a–z) and numbers (0–9). Maximum length is 7 characters. Leave the field empty and confirm to use the hardware default name.

The device name appears on the lock screen greeting and is used as the Bluetooth advertised device name.

Setting a PIN in the wizard

The PIN grid shows digits 0–9 arranged in a 3-column layout. Navigate with the directional buttons, press OK to select a digit, and press Back to delete the last digit. Once you have entered at least one digit you will be asked to confirm by entering the same PIN again.

The PIN is applied immediately — you do not need to reboot for the lock-screen PIN to become active. The PIN is also stored persistently so it survives power cycles.

Done page text:
If you changed your Flipper name → "Flipper will restart to apply your changes." The restart happens automatically when you press Finish.
If you only set a PIN (no name change) or made no changes → "Please join our Discord!" and the device does not restart.

Chapter 4

Desktop Overview

The FoxFW desktop is the idle screen you see after booting. FoxFW uses a fully custom Fox Theme with an all-orange UI — the stock Dolphin idle animation is intentionally absent and is replaced by a clean static screen. The status bar at the top shows the battery, clock (if enabled), and connection indicators.

Internet Connectivity Icon New

A small three-bar icon sits in the status bar immediately to the left of the battery indicator, showing whether an attached ESP32 companion board currently has a WiFi/internet connection.

Connected
Bold, solid ascending bars
Disconnected / Idle
Same bars, thin outline — not an error
Both states use the same three-ascending-bars shape on purpose, so they're clearly the same icon at a glance. The icon stays visible even when nothing is connected — it's a deliberate hint to anyone who hasn't tried the ESP32 companion apps yet that the feature exists at all.

The icon reflects whatever an ESP32 board wired to your Flipper's GPIO header last reported through FoxHub's WiFi menu, and FoxFW also runs a quiet background check roughly once a minute so the icon can't get stuck showing "connected" forever if the board is reflashed, unplugged, or powered off while no companion app is open to notice.

Requires Fox ESP32 Firmware. This icon — and the full suite of Fox ESP32 apps bundled with FoxFW (FoxHub, Chat, Portal, Detector, Flasher, and Chameleon) — need a companion ESP32 board wired to your Flipper's GPIO header and running Fox ESP32 Firmware. Flash it straight from the Flipper using the built-in Fox ESP32 Flasher app (no PC required), or via the Web Flasher. Source code and releases are at github.com/FoxFW/Fox_ESP32_FW. See the Fox ESP32 Apps section below for a full overview.

Fox Clock has its own Left/Right and Up/Down shortcuts once you're on it: ← Left turns the backlight off immediately; → Right turns it back on. Lock Menu (Up short)

The Lock Menu appears when you short-press Up on the desktop. It offers:

  • Lock — Lock the screen immediately.
  • Power Off — Shut down the device.
  • Reboot — Restart the device.
  • Reboot to DFU — Restart into USB firmware-flash mode (for qFlipper updates).

Lock Screen

When locked, the screen shows a padlock and the PIN entry grid will display when you press any key (if a PIN is set). Enter your PIN to unlock. If no PIN is set, any button press unlocks the device.

What's drawn on the lock screen (time, seconds, date, status bar, the "Back ×3 to unlock" hint) is configurable in Fox Settings → Security & Privacy → Lock Screen Display. If Allow Poweroff While Locked is ON, holding Back on the lock screen powers the device off without unlocking it first.

USB: No AutoLock — In Fox Settings → Security, the USB: No AutoLock setting suppresses the auto-lock timer while qFlipper is actively connected via USB. This prevents the device from locking and dropping your qFlipper session mid-transfer. When USB is disconnected, normal auto-lock timing resumes immediately.

Auto-lock and PIN: If your PIN is removed — or after a firmware update clears it — the auto-lock timer is automatically reset to Off on the next boot. Auto-locking without a PIN has no security value (the Back×3 bypass is trivially easy), so FoxFW enforces this reset silently and saves the corrected settings.
Wrong PIN attempts: If you enter the wrong PIN more times than the configured MAX Attempts limit, the On Exceed action triggers (see Fox Settings → Security & Privacy). If you are permanently locked out, email foxcustomfirmware@gmail.com.

Chapter 5

Fox File Browser FoxFW Exclusive

The Fox File Browser (FFB) replaces the stock Archive app as FoxFW's primary file management and launch tool. It is accessible from the desktop (Down short press, or any configured Favorite shortcut) and from within the firmware updater flow.

The home screen offers two options:

  • Browse SD Card — Navigate the full SD card file system.
  • Favorites — Jump directly to pinned files. (Only shown if you have at least one Favorite saved.)
If you have no Favorites saved, the home screen is skipped entirely and FFB opens directly in Browse mode. The home screen only appears once you have pinned at least one file.

Browse SD Card

The file browser lists all files and folders on your SD card. Navigate with Up / Down, open a folder with OK or Right, and go back with Left or Back.

The Left button on the home screen also directly opens the Favorites list if one exists.

Favorites

The Favorites list shows pinned files as two-line rows:

  • Line 1 (bold): The filename (e.g. update.fuf)
  • Line 2: The directory path, formatted as / folder / subfolder /

If the path hint is too long to fit, it scrolls when selected — bouncing left and right so you can read the full path. Unselected rows show a …suffix — the most specific (rightmost) part of the path that fits, preceded by ....

Favorites are stored in /ext/apps_data/ffb/favorites.txt. The file is created automatically if it does not exist.

File Actions

Press OK on any file in Browse or Favorites to open the action menu:

ActionFile typeWhat it does
Run in app NFC, Sub-GHz, Infrared, iButton, MP3, text, and other supported types Opens the matching app pre-loaded with the selected file. If more than one app can open that file type, an Open With menu lists the candidates by app name — pick one to launch it.
Install .fuf firmware update files Launches the firmware updater to install the selected firmware. The device will restart automatically to begin the update process.
Launch .fap external apps Runs the selected FAP application directly.

If Fox XBM Converter is installed, OK instead opens an Open With menu offering View (same reminder) or Fox XBM Converter (converts it back to .xbm).

Run in app currently covers: .sub/.rem (Sub-GHz), .nfc (NFC), .ir (Infrared), .rfid/.lfrfid (125 kHz RFID), .bad (Bad USB), .ibtn/.ibutton (iButton), .u2f (U2F), .js/.jss (JS Runner), .mp3 (MP3 Player), .txt/.csv (FlipNote — a full on-device text editor), .xbm (View — opens FFB's own built-in image viewer for the 128x64 screenshots the Back+Up shortcut saves, no separate app to launch), and .bmp (a screenshot already converted by Fox XBM Converter — View just shows a reminder, since FFB can't render it). If Fox XBM Converter is installed, both .xbm and .bmp instead offer it as a second Open With option alongside View. A file whose extension has no matching app (e.g. a bare .wav — the bundled MP3 Player only decodes MP3) doesn't get a Run entry at all, rather than offering an action that does nothing.

Chapter 6

Fox Settings

Fox Settings is found in the main App Menu under Settings. It contains all FoxFW-specific configuration. The menu remembers your scroll position while you navigate into sub-menus and back — it only resets to the top when you exit the app entirely.

Security & Privacy

The Security & Privacy screen is the heart of FoxFW's protection system. It covers PIN management, attempt limiting, the On Exceed action, automatic communication disconnect on lock, and auto-lock timers.

PIN Setup

The label changes based on current state: Set PIN (no PIN exists), Change PIN, and Remove PIN (once one is set).

PIN entry uses the digit grid — Up/Down moves between rows, Left/Right moves between columns, OK selects a digit, Back deletes the last digit. Confirm by pressing OK in the bottom-right cell once your minimum digit count is reached.

The PIN is applied immediately without requiring a reboot.

MAX Attempts
How many wrong PINs are allowed before the On Exceed action fires. Set to 0 to allow unlimited attempts (no lockout).
Range: 0 (unlimited) · 1 – 10
On Exceed
What happens when wrong PINs exceed MAX Attempts. Only shown (not N/A) when MAX Attempts is greater than 0.

Lock only — The device immediately reboots and on the next startup displays a "Locked!" screen; normal access via the device or qFlipper is blocked. Nothing on the SD card is touched — only the PIN lockout state changes.

Format SD — Same lockout behaviour as above, plus the SD card is fully formatted before the reboot. This is real, unrecoverable data loss (saved signals, NFC cards, wallpaper, everything on the card) — pick this only if you would rather lose your files than have them read by whoever is entering the wrong PIN.
Options: Lock only · Format SD
Recovery after On Exceed triggers: The device is locked, which also blocks qFlipper access. The fastest path back is a DFU firmware reinstall — your SD card files are unaffected only if you had On Exceed set to "Lock only." If "Format SD" was selected, the card was already wiped the moment the lockout triggered, before you ever get to the reinstall step. See the Lockout & Recovery section below for a step-by-step guide (you will need a computer and your SD card).
Allow Poweroff While Locked New
When ON, holding Back on the lock screen powers the device off directly — no PIN needed, since powering off doesn't expose any data. When OFF (default), the lock screen has no poweroff path; you must unlock first.
Options: OFF · ON

Disconnect Services

Controls which interfaces are disconnected when the device locks. All sub-options are disabled and shown as N/A while On Lock is OFF.

On Lock (master toggle)
Must be ON for any interface to disconnect on lock.
Options: OFF · ON
BLE
Drops any active Bluetooth Low Energy connection when the screen locks. The Flipper re-advertises automatically after unlocking.
Options: OFF · ON
GPIO
Acquires exclusive control of the hardware UART pins (USART & LPUART) on the GPIO header when locked, blocking external serial adapters and debuggers. Pins are released on unlock.
Options: OFF · ON
USB
Minimal — USB stays connected, GUI is locked. qFlipper buttons are inactive but the device stays visible. Fastest to unlock.

CLI + RPC — Physical USB connection stays alive but the active CLI/RPC session is closed. Reopen qFlipper after unlocking.

Full Disconnect — The CDC interface is torn down. Windows removes the COM port, qFlipper shows "Connect your Flipper." Maximum isolation. Best for unattended or high-security storage.
Choosing USB mode:
At desk, actively working → Minimal
Stepping away but staying plugged in → CLI + RPC
Storing, transporting, or handing over → Full Disconnect
Auto Lock Timer
How long the device stays idle before locking automatically.
Options: OFF · 10s · 15s · 30s · 60s · 90s · 2min · 5min · 10min
Auto Lock Disarm by USB
When ON, the auto-lock timer is suppressed while the Flipper is connected to USB — handy for desk sessions with qFlipper.
Options: OFF · ON

Custom Wallpaper

Replaces the desktop background with a custom 128 × 64 pixel monochrome image. FoxFW can store any number of these at once in a wallpapers folder on the root of the SD Card (path: /ext/wallpapers/) and lets you switch between them from Fox Settings without reconnecting the device.

If the folder is missing or empty, FoxFW automatically generates a default wallpaper inside it on first boot. This means a valid wallpaper is always available and selected by default.

FoxFW Wallpaper Painter New

The easiest way to make a wallpaper — draw it directly in your browser, no separate converter or image editor needed. The border and status bar are pre-locked so you can't draw somewhere it won't show up, and you can preview your drawing live on the device or save it straight to the SD card over USB (which also activates it immediately). It can also import your Flipper's currently active custom wallpaper straight into the editor, so you can tweak an existing one instead of starting from scratch.
FoxFW Wallpaper Converter

Prefer working offline in MS Paint? This older, standalone tool turns a .xbm wallpaper into a .bmp that you can edit in MS Paint (or any similar image editor), then converts it back to .xbm when you're ready to put it back on your SD card.

To use it: copy the wallpaper you want to edit into the same folder as the Wallpaper Converter and run it from there.

github.com/FoxFW/FoxFW-Wallpaper-Converter →
FoxFW Wallpaper Showcase New

Don't want to make your own? Browse community wallpapers and install one straight to your SD card over USB (this activates it immediately too), or just download the file and copy it over yourself.
Wallpaper Name
Lists every valid 128×64 .xbm file found in /ext/wallpapers/, alphabetically. Use < and > to pick which one is active — the desktop updates as soon as you select it.
Custom Wallpaper
Toggles the wallpaper on or off. The dolphin animation is hidden when a wallpaper is active — the desktop shows your artwork cleanly with the status bar and any running app UI drawn on top.
Options: OFF · ON
Quick switch: Hold Right on the desktop New

A hardcoded shortcut for cycling wallpapers without opening Fox Settings. Custom Wallpaper off → turns it on. On with only one wallpaper file → turns it back off (default Fox background). On with two or more files → advances to the next one alphabetically, wrapping back to the first after the last.
Live updates: if you overwrite a wallpaper file over USB (e.g. with qFlipper) while it's the active one, the desktop picks up the change automatically — no reboot or menu visit needed.

Creating your own wallpaper:

  • Your image must be exactly 128 × 64 px, monochrome (1-bit black and white), in XBM format.
  • The status bar occupies roughly the top 13 pixels — keep important artwork below that.
  • GIMP: Image → Scale Image → 128×64 → Image → Mode → Indexed (1-bit) → File → Export As → yourname.xbm
  • ImageMagick: convert input.png -resize 128x64! -monochrome yourname.xbm
  • Copy the file into the wallpapers folder on the root of your SD card, so it lives at /ext/wallpapers/yourname.xbm.
  • Select it under Wallpaper Name in Fox Settings and toggle Custom Wallpaper ON.
Validation: Only files that are valid 128×64 XBM images show up in the Wallpaper Name list. If a file you copied over isn't appearing, re-export using one of the methods above and ensure the output dimensions are exactly 128×64 pixels.

Changes the device's display name. The name appears on the lock screen greeting and is broadcast as the Bluetooth device name.

  • Allowed characters: letters and numbers only (A–Z, a–z, 0–9).

Tip: A unique name makes your Flipper easy to identify in qFlipper and when nearby Flippers exchange information.

ESP32 UART New

A single global preference for which GPIO pin pair the Fox ESP32 apps (FoxHub, Terminal, Flasher, and the built-in UART Terminal) default to for talking to an ESP32 companion board.

ESP32 UART
Choosing a pair here updates the shared default immediately. Each of those apps still has its own "UART Pins" option in its own connection settings screen for a one-off override — but changing it in any one of them (including here) updates this same shared preference, so the others pick up the new default the next time you open their connection settings.
Options: 13/14 · 15/16
Fox ESP32 Detector isn't in this list — it always auto-scans both pin pairs when detecting a board, so there's nothing for it to remember.

VGM Options New

Sets the foreground/background color used when this device's screen is streamed to a computer over USB/BLE (qFlipper and similar screen-mirroring tools). Purely cosmetic on the receiving end — has no effect on the device's own display.

Foreground / Background
Cycle Left/Right through a list of named colors, or one of three special modes: Default (plain black/white, stock behavior), Rainbow (the receiving app animates its own rainbow), or RgbMod (mirrors this device's live RGB backlight color, including the current rainbow/wave animation if one is running — requires RGB Backlight to be installed).

Menu Style

Menu Style
Controls the layout of the main Menu only — the app list you get from the Desktop's center button. Submenus and settings lists elsewhere in the firmware keep the same rounded-box styling under all four options; only Classic switches those back to plain, unboxed lists.

Classic — the original Flipper vertical list: one icon centered on screen at a time, with the previous/next item faded above/below and a scrollbar on the right. Up/Down/Left/Right all step through the list.
Fox Theme — a 3×2 grid of double-row button boxes, six visible at once, orange FoxFW styling. All four directions move between cells.
Carousel — a horizontal row of icon tiles (the current item, one before it, up to four after), with the selected tile drawn larger and a "Start" banner + scrolling name underneath; side tiles show icon only. Device name top-left, dotted scrollbar along the bottom. Only Left/Right move through it; Up/Down do nothing. Only this main Menu ever uses this style — no other app's own internal menu is affected, and Carousel has no settings of its own beyond this picker.
Slider New — a horizontal row of icon tiles that wraps around in both directions (two items before the current one, two after), with the selected tile shown larger in a bold frame with a notched top edge and a scrolling name underneath. Dotted scrollbar along the bottom. Only Left/Right move through it; Up/Down do nothing, same as Carousel.
Options: Classic · Fox Theme · Carousel · Slider

Sub-GHz

The Sub-GHz app lets your Flipper interact with radio-frequency devices between ~300 MHz and 928 MHz — garage doors, gate openers, weather stations, tyre-pressure sensors, key fobs, alarm remotes, and many more. FoxFW adds a Waterfall display, an in-app RAW editor, a Modulation Analyzer, and per-protocol enable/disable toggles.

On launch you're asked to pick a Sub-Ghz Mode — see Sub-Ghz Mode below. Everything else in this chapter (Read, Read RAW, Saved Files, Protocol List, Radio Settings, and so on) works identically no matter which mode you picked; only the set of protocols loaded changes.

Legal notice: Only transmit signals you are authorised to send. Replaying remotes you own is generally fine; transmitting on licensed frequencies without authorization is not. Know your local regulations.

Read Enhanced

Opens on a Start screen — press OK to begin listening for an incoming signal. Unlike the original Read screen (which decodes every protocol continuously in real time), Garage/Gate/Other's Read now records the incoming signal raw first and only runs it through the protocol decoders once the transmission has actually finished — the listening screen looks and behaves like Read RAW's own recording screen. A signal has to clear the RSSI Threshold set in Receiver Config before Read starts treating it as an actual transmission, so ambient RF noise alone won't trigger a capture. If the signal decodes successfully you're taken straight to its info screen to save or discard it; if nothing matches, a "No Match" screen offers Back (return to the Start screen) or Save (keep the raw capture and load it into the RAW editor). Nothing is written to SD card unless you choose to save.

Read RAW Enhanced

Records everything the radio receives as a timed sequence of high/low edges without attempting to decode. Use this when the protocol is unknown, not yet supported, or uses rolling codes.

FoxFW enhancements to Read RAW:

  • Recording timer — A timer in the top-right corner of the waveform display shows how long the current recording has been running. The timer stays visible after recording stops so you always know the length of the last capture.

Fiat V1 (Hitag2) key Updated — Fiat V1 signals are secured with a Hitag2-derived rolling code that also depends on a hidden 18-bit "epoch" counter. Modulation Analyzer FoxFW Exclusive

New in FoxFW. The Modulation Analyzer does not exist in stock Flipper firmware.

The Modulation Analyzer solves the complementary problem to the Frequency Analyzer: you know the frequency but you don't know the modulation. It rapidly cycles through all enabled modulation presets at a fixed frequency, recording any preset whose RSSI rises above a detection threshold as a "hit." After scanning, select a hit and press OK to open the Receiver pre-configured for that exact frequency and modulation.

Starting a scan:

The app opens on a Set Frequency digit-entry screen — Left/Right move the cursor, Up/Down change the selected digit, OK starts scanning at that frequency. It cycles through every enabled preset at roughly 120 ms each, watching for any preset whose RSSI rises above the trigger threshold.

Controls while scanning:

Which presets get scanned: in Automotive mode, the built-in Modulations menu (see below) has a per-preset ON/OFF list that applies here too — the Modulation Analyzer only cycles through presets you've left enabled there. Modulations Automotive

A separate menu item in the Sub-GHz main list (distinct from the Modulation Analyzer above) listing every modulation preset with an ON/OFF toggle, built dynamically from the live preset file so any preset you add to setting_user shows up automatically. Selecting the top row toggles "Enable All" / "Disable All" in one step. At least one preset must stay enabled.

Garage/Gate/Other mode doesn't have this menu — its preset needs are already minimal and tied to whichever Protocol Group is selected (only Group 8 "Germany" needs anything beyond the everyday AM650/FM476 presets), so a separate manual toggle screen was redundant and has been removed there.
Why disable presets: both the Modulation Analyzer and the frequency sweep use this same enabled-preset list — trimming it to the presets you actually care about makes each scan cycle faster and cuts down on false-positive hits.

System UX — Bounce Scroll & Text Rendering FoxFW Exclusive

FoxFW replaces the stock single-direction text scroll with a bounce-scroll system applied throughout the entire firmware. Any label that is too long to fit in its display area will:

  1. Pause briefly showing the start of the text.
  2. Smoothly slide left to reveal the end of the text.
  3. Pause at the right end.
  4. Slide back to the left — and repeat.

This affects the App Menu, all Settings menus, the Sub-GHz Protocol List, the Fox File Browser Favorites list path hints, and every other scrollable text element in the firmware.

In the Fox File Browser Favorites list specifically:

  • Selected row: The path hint (line 2) bounce-scrolls so you can read the full path of both files even if they share the same filename.
  • Unselected rows: Show …suffix — the most specific end of the path that fits, preceded by ..., so you can always see which folder the file is in.

Chapter 9

Screenshot Tool FoxFW Exclusive

Capture exactly what's on screen, from anywhere in the firmware — no computer, no qFlipper, no cables. Hold Back + Up together for about one second on any screen (the lock screen, the Desktop, any app, any menu) and FoxFW saves the current display to your SD card.

A confirmation box with corner accents pops up on screen for one second ("Screenshot" / camera icon / Saved), while the LED flashes blue twice with a short vibration on the second flash. If saving fails (usually a missing or full SD card), you'll get FoxFW's standard error notification instead and nothing else on screen changes.
  • Where it saves: /Screenshots/ on the SD card, created automatically the first time you use it.
  • Filename: Screenshot_<app>_YYYYMMDD_HHMMSS.xbm — the date, time, and the currently running app's name (e.g. Screenshot_subghz_garage_20260905_073225.xbm), or desktop in place of the app name when captured from the idle home/lock screen. The timestamp means repeated captures never overwrite each other.
  • Format: a standard .xbm bitmap — a plain-text file readable by GIMP, ImageMagick, and most image tools, and the exact same format FoxFW's own Custom Wallpaper feature uses. A screenshot can be dropped straight into /wallpapers and used as a wallpaper if you like what you captured, or viewed full-screen from Fox File Browser's own built-in viewer (press OK on it, then View).
  • Converting screenshots New — the Fox XBM Converter app (Apps → Fox) turns a saved .xbm screenshot into a monochrome .bmp you can edit on a PC (MS Paint or similar), then convert back to .xbm when you're done. It only browses /Screenshots/. From FFB, pressing OK on any .xbm or .bmp file in that folder offers it directly in the Open With menu alongside View, once it's installed.
Back and Up are captured for as long as you hold them together, so the screenshot combo takes over cleanly — it won't also trigger Back's normal "go back" or "exit app" behavior underneath while you're holding for a capture. Release both keys and everything goes back to normal.

Chapter 10

Installing Firmware Updates

There are four ways to install a FoxFW update:

Method 1 — via Web Installer New (recommended)

  1. Connect your Flipper to your computer via USB.
  2. Open the FoxFW Web Installer in Chrome or Edge on desktop.
  3. Choose Standard Update and click Connect Flipper, selecting your device from the browser dialog.
  4. Click Install Now — the installer downloads the firmware and transfers it automatically, no software install required.
  5. The Flipper will reboot. On first boot after the update the Fox Setup Wizard runs.
Tip: No qFlipper install needed — everything runs in the browser. See Chapter 2 for full details on the Web Installer.

Method 2 — via qFlipper

  1. Connect your Flipper to your computer via USB.
  2. Open qFlipper (v1.3.3 or later).
  3. Click Install from file and select the FoxFW .tgz update package.
  4. qFlipper will flash the firmware and copy resources automatically.
  5. The Flipper will reboot. On first boot after the update the Fox Setup Wizard runs.

Method 3 — via Fox File Browser (wireless / SD-card update)

  1. Copy the .fuf firmware update file to your SD card (any folder — Favorites or a dedicated folder is recommended).
  2. Open Fox File Browser from the desktop.
  3. Navigate to the .fuf file and press OK.
  4. Select Install from the action menu.
  5. The firmware updater launches and the device restarts to apply the update.
Tip: Pin your .fuf file to FFB Favorites for one-tap access — ideal if you regularly test new builds.

Method 4 — via Fox Update Downloader (fully wireless, no PC or USB)

If you already have a wired ESP32 running Fox ESP32 Firmware, Fox Update Downloader can check GitHub, download, and install a FoxFW update entirely on-device — the ESP32 acts as the internet connection your Flipper doesn't otherwise have.

  1. Make sure your ESP32 is wired up and joined to a WiFi network (see the Fox ESP32 Apps chapter if you haven't set this up yet).
  2. Open Fox Update Downloader and select Fox Custom Firmware.
  3. Wait for the check to finish. If an update is available, press Download.
  4. Once it finishes downloading, press Install — the device restarts automatically to apply it, same as any other method.
Not ready to install yet? Press Later instead of Install — the downloaded update stays on your SD card, and a Restart button on the following screen installs it whenever you come back, without downloading it again. The same app can also update your Fox ESP32 Firmware — see the Fox ESP32 Firmware guide for that side of it, including picking your board.
After an update: The Fox Setup Wizard runs on the first boot after every firmware update. Your PIN, name, settings, and files are all preserved. The wizard simply confirms your preferences for the new build.
Slow verification at ~11%: During "Install from file" via qFlipper, the progress bar may pause for an extended period (30 seconds or more) while verifying resources.ths. This is normal — FoxFW includes a larger resource bundle than stock firmware. qFlipper will eventually continue. If qFlipper times out and reports an error, the most reliable alternative is to copy the update .tgz directly to your SD card using a card reader, then use the Fox File Browser to install it from the device itself — this bypasses the USB verification step entirely.

Chapter 11

Lockout & Recovery

I forgot my PIN

If you remember your PIN but keep entering it wrong, stop — every failed attempt counts toward the MAX Attempts limit. Once the limit is reached, the On Exceed action fires and the device reboots to a "Locked!" screen. At that point normal access via the device or qFlipper is blocked. See below for recovery options.

I am completely locked out

If you cannot get back into the device on your own, you have two options:

  1. Reinstall the firmware via qFlipper DFU Mode. Boot your Flipper into DFU mode (there are several methods for this that should be common knowledge to you by now). In DFU Mode, connect your Flipper via USB, open qFlipper, and use Install from file to flash flipper-z-f7-full-local.dfu. This will restore normal access. Your SD card files (saved signals, NFC cards, etc.) are not affected by a firmware reinstall — but if On Exceed was set to "Format SD" rather than "Lock only", the card was already formatted the moment the lockout triggered, before you get to this step. A firmware reinstall can't undo that.
  2. Contact support. Email us and we will provide recovery instructions specific to your situation.
